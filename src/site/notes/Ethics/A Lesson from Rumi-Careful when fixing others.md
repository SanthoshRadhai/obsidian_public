# Why We Need to Stop Fixing Others: A Lesson from Rumi

  

Have you ever found yourself completely consumed by someone else's problems? We often act as the unsolicited therapist for our friends, family, or even distant relatives, trying to fix their lives while completely ignoring our own. In a video by *Daughter of Iran*, creator Sara shares an insightful story by the 13th-century Persian poet Rumi that perfectly illustrates why we need to mind our own business.

  

### The Foolish Man and the Bones

Sara shares an allegory from Rumi's *Masnavi Manavi* about a foolish man traveling with Jesus. As they walk, they stumble upon a pit of old bones. The man begs Jesus, *"Jesus could you please teach me that glorious name let me say it over the bones and bring them back to life I want to do something good."*

  

Jesus immediately warns him against it. He explains that it takes more than just knowing the words, saying, *"Be still... this is not your task your breath and words are not fit for what you ask... suppose you hold the staff ready to command what you do not have is Moses hand."*

  

### A Dead Soul Mending Strangers' Bones

When the man persists, pleading, *"If I'm not worthy of the secret then you do it... I just want to see that,"* Jesus turns to God in disbelief. He asks, *"What's wrong with this guy I can literally do anything to make his life better... why doesn't this sick man ask for a cure."*

  

Eventually, Jesus speaks the name over the pit. The bones assemble and resurrect into a massive black lion. The lion leaps up and kills the foolish man. When Jesus asks the lion why he killed him but didn't eat him, the lion replies, *"Because he wasn't assigned as my food... I wasn't supposed to be alive this was all to make a point if you're not ready to receive even if you beg and you receive what's not yours it will destroy you."*

  

### Look in the Mirror

The moral of this brutal story is simple: stop worrying about fixing other people's lives and start looking inward. Instead of feeding our ego by playing the savior for others, we must acknowledge and heal our own flaws. As Sara quotes from Rumi, *"You've wept for others your whole life through, now sit for a while and cry for you."*

  

Next time you feel the urge to intervene in someone else's drama, remember the foolish man and the black lion. It might just be the perfect reminder to mind your own business and tend to your own soul first.
